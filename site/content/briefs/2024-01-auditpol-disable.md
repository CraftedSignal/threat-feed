This threat brief focuses on the abuse of `auditpol.exe`, a legitimate Windows command-line tool, by adversaries to disable or modify audit policies. The activity involves the execution of `auditpol.exe` with the `/set` command-line argument, specifically targeting the disabling of audit policy categories or sub-categories. This behavior is typically observed during the post-exploitation phase of an attack, where threat actors attempt to evade detection by reducing the amount of security-related telemetry generated by the compromised system. Red Teams and adversaries use this technique to limit the visibility of their actions and maintain persistence within the network. Disabling audit policies can significantly hinder incident response efforts and prolong the attacker's dwell time.

## Attack Chain

1.  The attacker gains initial access to the system via methods such as exploiting a vulnerability or using stolen credentials.
2.  The attacker executes `auditpol.exe` with the `/set` parameter to modify the audit policy.
3.  The command includes arguments to disable specific audit categories or subcategories (e.g., `/success:disable`, `/failure:disable`).
4.  The attacker uses specific command-line parameters with `auditpol.exe` to target particular logging functions.
5.  The Windows Event Logging or Sysmon logging is effectively reduced, which impacts detection capabilities.
6.  The attacker performs actions such as lateral movement, data exfiltration, or malware installation, with reduced risk of detection.
7.  The attacker maintains persistence on the system, leveraging the disabled audit policies to conceal their ongoing activity.

## Impact

Successful execution of this attack can lead to significant disruption of security monitoring and incident response capabilities. Disabling audit policies reduces the visibility into attacker activities, allowing them to operate undetected for extended periods. This can lead to data breaches, system compromise, and financial loss. The number of affected systems can vary depending on the scope of the attack, but even a single compromised system with disabled audit policies can serve as a foothold for further attacks within the network.

## Recommendation

*   Deploy the Sigma rule `Detect Auditpol Usage to Disable Logging` to your SIEM and tune for your environment to detect the use of auditpol to disable logging.
*   Monitor process creation events (Sysmon Event ID 1 or Windows Event Log Security 4688) for executions of `auditpol.exe` with the `/set` argument.
*   Investigate any instances of `auditpol.exe` execution with the `/set` argument, as documented in the rule and description, to determine if the changes are authorized.
*   Review and harden default audit policies to ensure adequate logging coverage.
*   Enable Sysmon process-creation logging to activate the rules above.
